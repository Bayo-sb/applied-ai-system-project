# Draft: task table from the AAFP-AAHA Feline Life Stage Guidelines

DRAFT FOR REVIEW. Not part of the knowledge base yet, and not for retrieval.
General guidance, not veterinary advice.

Source: Hoyumpa Vogt A, Rodan I, Brown M, et al. "AAFP-AAHA Feline Life Stage Guidelines."
J Am Anim Hosp Assoc 2010; 46:70-85. Local copy: `assets/aaha-aafp-feline-life-stage-guidelines.pdf`
(not committed). Quotes below are exact. Page numbers come from text extraction and may be off by one;
check them against the PDF before citing.

Priority column: what the proposed rule (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW from the source's wording) would give.
The source never uses these words. Every priority below is my interpretation, for you to accept or change.

## 1. Life stages defined by the source (Table 1, pp. 73-77)

| Stage | Age as printed |
|---|---|
| Kitten | 0-6 m |
| Junior | 7 m-2 y |
| Adult | 3-6 y |
| Mature | 7-10 y |
| Senior | 11-14 y |
| Geriatric | 15+ y |

The source says: "Distinct life stages (age groups) in cats are not well defined" and "any age groupings are
inevitably arbitrary demarcations along a spectrum, and not absolutes." (p. 71)

Gaps in the printed ranges: nothing is stated for an age such as 2 y 6 m (between "2 y" and "3 y") or
6 m 15 d. The system needs a rule for these. I will not guess one.

## 4. Issues for you to decide

A. The source is written for veterinarians. Most of its numbers are vet-visit and vet-treatment schedules,
not daily owner tasks. It gives no numbers for walks, feeding times, litter cleaning or play minutes.
A "daily plan" built only from this document would be mostly vet appointments and reminders.

B. The source never ranks tasks. Words such as "minimum", "critical", "important" and "recommended" would
map to HIGH or MEDIUM, but that mapping is the system's rule, not the source's.

C. Deworming and heartworm prevention involve medication. Our guardrail refuses medication and dosage.
Proposed handling: the task is "Vet visit for parasite control" with the schedule cited, and the system
never states a product or a dose. You decide whether that is acceptable.

D. The play study is one study's result. Turning it into "play 3 times a day for 10-15 minutes" would
treat a finding as a recommendation. Proposed: do not use it for tasks.

E. Ages between the printed ranges (see section 1) need a rule.

F. The Cornell handout (separate PDF) mentions cleaning the litter box once a day. It is another source
and has not been checked yet.
